what_i_learned: |   
  Day two of the symposium and the one everything has been building toward all summer.
  We went first, which turned out to be a blessing. Getting the presentation out of the way early meant we could settle in     and actually enjoy the rest of the day rather than sitting with nerves through everyone else's session. Going first also     meant we got a fresh audience and a lot of thoughtful feedback. The standout moment was a question from Dr. Nyarko, the      program director. There is something particularly validating about the person who leads the entire program taking a          genuine interest in your research. It confirmed that the work we put in over these ten weeks landed the way we intended it   to.

  After our presentation we settled in to watch our teammates present their final research. Seeing everyone's work in its      finished form after spending the whole summer in the same building, hearing about each other's progress in bits and          pieces, was a genuinely moving experience. Every group came in with a problem and left with something real to show for it.   That is not a small thing.

  The hackathon presentation was the other highlight of the day. I presented our working prototype to the judges and the       reception was positive, they seemed genuinely intrigued by what we had built. Knowing how little sleep went into getting     that prototype across the finish line made it all the more satisfying to stand in front of the judges with something that    actually worked. I am looking forward to seeing how we place. More broadly, this symposium was a fitting end to a program    that pushed me further than I expected. The research, the skills, the people, and the experience are all things I plan to    carry directly into my senior thesis and beyond.
