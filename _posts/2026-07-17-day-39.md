what_i_learned: |   
  The biggest milestone was completing the training of our baseline model, which finished at 20 epochs with a validation F1    score of 0.78. F1 is the right metric to lean on here given the class imbalance in the xBD dataset, and 0.78 gives us a      concrete benchmark to compare our other architectures against as we move into final evaluation.

  Before diving into evaluation I made a model swap in the FastAPI app. The most recent model we trained showed stronger       performance on the extremes — No Damage and Destroyed classifications — but underperformed compared to the initial model     on Minor and Major Damage buildings. That tradeoff matters because Minor and Major are the hardest classes to get right,     both for our model and in the published xBD literature. For now the initial model stays in the app since it handles the      full damage spectrum more consistently, but it's a useful data point on how different training runs affect class-level       performance differently.

  The rest of the day went into evaluation work. With three models now in the pipeline — the baseline, the Siamese network,    and the ViT — the challenge is presenting the results in a way that actually communicates something meaningful to an         audience that isn't steeped in the technical details. I worked on pulling together the normalized confusion matrix,          precision-recall curves, and prediction confidence distributions across all three models into a format that's simpler to     read and easier to explain. The goal is for someone looking at our evaluation results to immediately understand which        model performs best, where each one falls short, and why those differences matter for real-world deployment.
