what_i_learned: |   
  Continued working on the final symposium presentation today, and it ended up being more involved than just filling in slides. A significant portion of the day went into revisiting our previous work and decisions,          specifically why we made certain architectural and methodological choices rather than following established benchmarks. That kind of reflection is important for a final presentation because an audience will ask why, not   just what. Going back through those decisions forced me to relearn some things I thought I had fully internalized. The distinction between building a model from scratch and fine-tuning an existing one is a good example,   concepts that feel solid when you're in the middle of the work can get fuzzy when you have to articulate them precisely to someone else. Taking the time to sharpen those definitions now will make a real difference when    we're standing in front of the symposium audience. It's a humbling but useful part of the research process. The deeper you go into a project, the more you realize how much nuance sits underneath the surface level          understanding. On the technical side, we restarted training on the Siamese network after successfully re-obtaining our pre-disaster images, which had been a blocker for a while. Getting that training back underway is a    meaningful step, the Siamese architecture is central to our approach of comparing pre and post disaster imagery, and having it trained properly will strengthen both our results and our evaluation metrics heading into      the symposium.
