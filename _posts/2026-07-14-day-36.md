what_i_learned: |   
  Kept pushing on our deliverables today across a few different fronts.
  I continued testing the FastAPI web app with multiple satellite images, running real xBD patches through the full pipeline to make    sure the app holds up consistently across different inputs and disaster types. At this stage of the program, thorough testing is      what separates a demo that works once from something that can be reliably presented at the symposium. Each round of testing either    confirms the app is solid or surfaces something worth fixing before we get there.

  We also made progress on the literature review section of the paper. The literature review is one of those sections that requires     more care than it might seem — it's not just about summarizing what other researchers have done, but about positioning our own work   in the context of what already exists and making clear what gap we're addressing.

  The highlight of the day was a short session with a group of high school teachers where we walked them through our project: what      we've built, how it works, and the real-world impact we're aiming for. Explaining technical research to a non-technical audience is   its own skill, and it was a good exercise in communicating the work clearly and accessibly. The conversation was a good reminder of   why the project matters beyond the research itself — faster, more accurate damage assessment after a disaster has real consequences   for real people, and being able to articulate that clearly is just as important as the technical work behind it.
