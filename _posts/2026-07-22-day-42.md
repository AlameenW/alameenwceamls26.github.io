what_i_learned: |   
  Today was a long one, but a good one.

  Started the day finalizing the presentation with my teammates and running through our delivery a few times before the formal practice session with SAIRI staff. Rehearsing   as a team is a different experience from practicing individually — you start to notice things like pacing between speakers, how transitions feel, and whether the overall    narrative flows the way you intend it to. We ran through it a couple of times before feeling ready enough to present.

  The session with SAIRI staff was useful. The feedback was constructive — minor formatting changes to the slides and a note on delivery, specifically around speaking more    naturally and avoiding verbatim recitation. That last point is something most presenters struggle with. When you've spent weeks building something and then written out      exactly what you want to say about it, the temptation is to just read it back. But the best presentations feel like a conversation, not a script. That is the standard I     am holding myself to heading into the actual symposium. I will keep working on my portion until it feels natural.

  After all of that, we headed out for the SAIRI tie-dye field day. By that point I was running on fumes after a full day of presentation prep, but it was exactly the kind    of break the day needed. It was also my first time doing tie-dye, so I walked away with something I actually made with my hands which was a nice change of pace from         staring at slides and code all day.
