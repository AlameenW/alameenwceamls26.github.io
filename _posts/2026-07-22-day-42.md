what_i_learned: |   
  Continued building out the final symposium presentation today with a focus on making the technical content actually         digestible for an audience that may not be deeply familiar with the work.
  The methodology section took up most of the morning. Breaking down three different architectures, the baseline ResNet-50,   the Siamese network, and the ViT, in a way that is clear, accurate, and presentable without losing the substance is         genuinely challenging. It required going back through each architecture and thinking carefully about how to explain not     just what each model does, but why we chose it and what role it plays in the overall pipeline.

  I also revisited the data splitting process and spent time relearning how the data loader works under the hood. These are   the kinds of details that are easy to gloss over when you're in the middle of building, but     that come back up the       moment    someone in the audience asks a pointed question. Having a solid grasp of every step in the pipeline, not just     the parts I   personally built, is important going into the final presentation.

  On the results side, I compiled the evaluation results for our baseline model and added them to the slides. Seeing the      numbers in the context of the full presentation helped reinforce how each piece of the project connects to the next.

  We divided responsibilities for the remaining slides and I spent the latter part of the day practicing the intro portion    of the presentation specifically. Delivery matters just as much as content, and getting comfortable with the opening sets   the tone for everything that follows.
