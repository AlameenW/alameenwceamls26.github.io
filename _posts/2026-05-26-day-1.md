what_i_learned: |
  We kicked off with a simple framing: research is contributing something new that has never been done before. Evaluation,     on the other hand, is checking whether something works the way you intended. The two are related, but they're not the same   thing. The potato salad recipe example actually made this land. 

  Core concepts I learned
  There are three variables every research should have:
  Independent variable — what you deliberately change. In an AI context, this could be using curated training data vs. fetching raw from the internet.
  Dependent variable — what you measure as a result. Think: weekly study hours (Independent Variable) → test score (Dependent Variable).
  Control group — the group you don't touch. It exists purely as a baseline so the comparison means something.

  Exploratory vs. applied research
  Exploratory research is about building and trying something new — developing methods that don't exist yet. Applied     research takes what's already known and uses it to build something new in a different context. 

  What makes a good research question?
  Specificity. A research question has to be very, very specific. Broad questions make for broad (useless) conclusions. The    narrower your question, the more defensible your answer — and the more useful it is to anyone who tries to build on it.
